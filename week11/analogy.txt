The fill-in-the-blank map (Part 4) produced a significantly sharper and semantic-driven 
geometry than the sentiment map (Part 2). The sentiment classifier map collapsed words 
onto a single dimensional axis of binary polarity (positive vs. negative). For instance, 
under sentiment training, terrible yields words exclusively tied to negative sentiment reviews 
such as annoying, lacks, and disappointment. In contrast, the fill-in-the-blank map learns 
context window placement, clustering words by their functional and grammatical roles. Re-querying 
terrible on the fill-in-the-blank embedding exposes an intensity axis rather than a strict 
sentiment split, bringing descriptive intensity synonyms like horrible, lousy, and pathetic into 
close proximity alongside high-intensity praise terms like brilliant, marvelous, and masterful. 
This contextual mapping allows vector operations like terrible + good - bad to yield meaningful 
relational outputs (horrible, lousy), whereas performing vector arithmetic on the sentiment 
embedding collapses into arbitrary sentiment points.
