In our autograd framework, implementing a forward operation without its backward branch breaks 
the chain rule at that exact node. If we deleted the mm (matrix multiplication) backward branch 
in tensor.py, the forward pass would execute normally without raising any errors or warnings. The 
network would still compute predictions and produce a loss value. However, during loss.backward(), 
the gradient propagation would hit the mm node and silently stop. Because mm never calls 
.backward() on its creators (input and self.weight), every upstream weight and bias parameter 
feeding into that layer would keep grad = None. In our framework, optim.step() would immediately 
crash with an AttributeError when trying to access p.grad.data; if guarded against None, those 
weights would simply never update, leaving the model frozen and incapable of learning.   

This is precisely the trap Vex fell into: keeping the forward "power" to generate outputs while 
stripping away the backward "price" that allows errors to correct the system. To catch this defect 
before shipping, we rely on numeric gradient checking (using central differences to verify that 
analytic gradients match numerical perturbations) paired with mandatory PR reviews verifying that 
every forward operation has a complete backward counterpart.
